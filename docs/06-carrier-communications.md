# Carrier and Telecom Communication Guide

## Purpose

This document outlines a disciplined, legal, and security-minded process for working with carriers or telecom operators when a suspected SIM-swap or number-takeover event is involved.

## Principles

- work through official, authorized channels only
- verify identity and authorization before discussing account details
- keep customer privacy and minimal necessary disclosure in force
- collect evidence before escalation
- document contact names, case IDs, and timestamps

## When to escalate

Escalate to the carrier or telecom partner when:
- a user reports a suspicious or unauthorized number transfer
- the phone number was recently changed without confirmation
- a support case suggests the user’s mobile identity was compromised
- there is risk of financial fraud or account takeover tied to the mobile number

## What to provide

Keep the information limited to what is required for investigation:
- customer account or case ID
- impacted phone number or SIM identifier
- time window of suspicious activity
- incident summary and known fraud indicators
- contact channel for the verified customer

## What not to provide

- do not reveal unrelated user or account details without authorization
- do not discuss support data beyond the specific incident
- do not share internal security notes with untrusted third parties

## Verification standard for carrier outreach

Before discussing a customer account with a carrier, ensure:
- the request is tied to a valid security case
- the user or representative is authorized
- the data disclosure is necessary and limited
- the carrier contact is official and verified

## Response expectation

Ask for:
- confirmation of whether a port-out, SIM swap, or line transfer occurred
- timestamps and source information of the event
- whether a secure verification or notification workflow was triggered
- any fraud flags or changes to account state

## Evidence handoff package

Include:
- affected customer account identifier
- timeline of suspicious access or recovery events
- whether the user reported compromise or social engineering
- any fraud evidence or ticket numbers
- known account risk controls that failed or were bypassed

## Internal handling

Keep the investigation separate from normal support routing. The case should be tracked by incident ID and assigned to a security or fraud owner.
